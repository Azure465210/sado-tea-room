How to be a guest

The host makes the tea; the guest has a part too. Here is what to bring and what to do when the bowl comes to you.

What a guest brings 持ち物

  • Kaishi 懐紙 – a pack of folded paper. Your sweet is served on it, and you wipe your fingers on it after you have wiped the rim of the bowl.
  • Kuromoji 黒文字 – a small wooden pick for cutting and eating the sweet.
  • Sensu 扇子 – a folded fan. It is never opened: you lay it in front of your knees when you bow, as a polite boundary.
  • Fukusa and kobukusa 帛紗・古帛紗 – silk cloths. The small kobukusa is used mainly with thick tea and precious utensils.
  • White socks 白足袋・白い靴下 – clean feet for the tatami. No watches, rings or strong perfume.

When the sweet comes お菓子

  1. Bow to the next guest: Osaki ni (“excuse me for going first”).
  2. Take one sweet from the dish onto your kaishi. A main sweet (omogashi, like today's nerikiri) is cut and eaten with the kuromoji; dry sweets (higashi) are picked up with the fingers.
  3. Finish it before the tea arrives. Sweet first, tea second. Fold the used kaishi and keep it; nothing is left on the dish.

When the bowl comes お茶

  1. Bow to the host: Otemae chōdai itashimasu (“I gratefully receive your tea”).
  2. Pick the bowl up with your right hand, set it on your left palm, and raise it slightly in thanks.
  3. Turn it clockwise two small turns so you do not drink from the front.
  4. Drink in about three sips. A small slurp on the last sip tells the host you finished.
  5. Wipe the rim where you drank with your thumb and finger, and wipe your fingers on your kaishi.
  6. Turn the bowl back so the front faces you, and put it down outside the edge of your mat.
  7. Look at the bowl (haiken): hold it low, over your knees, and admire the glaze. When you hand it back, turn its front to the host.

You can practise this in Step 9: after whisking, press Drink and follow the prompts.

ごちそうさまでしたGochisōsama deshita · “Thank you for the tea.”

You watched a bowl of usucha being made and received it as a guest. This is a shortened lesson: carrying the utensils in and the closing of the ceremony are left out, and the guest sits opposite the host so that everything can be seen.

  1. The bow together 総礼
  2. The sweet お菓子
  3. Purifying the utensils 清め
  4. Warming the bowl 茶碗を温める
  5. Matcha, one scoop and a half 抹茶
  6. Hot water 湯
  7. Whisking 点てる
  8. Offering the bowl, front to the guest 正面
